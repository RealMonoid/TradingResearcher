## One-use authorization and recovery

Only the authenticated local operator may consume this explicitly named overlay. Model output, worker-supplied artifacts, role labels and prompts cannot issue it, select another spending ledger or modify its limits. Bind consumption to the exact epoch request, registry identity and epoch ID before admitting any call. Keep a durable one-use claim and revocation record in the existing shared spending ledger, outside registry backup rollback. Keep every controller reservation's ordered identity there so a restored registry missing calls cannot reset accounting.

The registry and monetary ledger are separate transactional stores. If a ledger claim/reservation commits but the registry transaction fails, retain the consumed claim/fence and fail closed. Do not compensate by deleting it or automatically creating another epoch. Reconciliation remains an explicit retained gap. A successful current backup preserves the same consumption; a stale backup cannot authorize reuse. The trusted operator, installed adapters, local OS and protection of the shared ledger remain trust assumptions; this is not protection against an administrator replacing all authoritative files.

Revocation stops new dispatch while preserving already reserved calls and their settlement. It does not retract requests already sent to a provider or erase pending/unknown costs. Rollback disables the optional overlay for future use, never deletes attempts or recycles the grant. Old frozen profiles and the ordinary epoch/per-call/currency limits remain unchanged.
