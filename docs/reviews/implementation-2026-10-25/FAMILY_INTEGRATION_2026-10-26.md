## Implemented consuming path

It derives the family count, base block length, delta and direction from that plan. Two through eight distinct synthetic responses use one shared template's q, w, condition/missingness grid, horizon and instruments, with independently declared existing effect scenarios. A shared mask sequence and common stationary latent channel produce a joint law; per-response/instrument latents and choices have separate named streams. All states evolve even at ineligible times. These synthetic components do not claim conflicting truths for the original market AST.

Each generated response runs through the existing stationary-bootstrap/Monte Carlo calculator, with every prescribed draw and actual family noncoverage. Shared bootstrap paths preserve aligned resampling. The full response vector is one outer world. Null, boundary and alternative events inspect only the relevant truth components; missing required intervals cannot yield successful coverage. A known union error is retained even when another member is unavailable. Technical/model failures are unavailable; undefined-information outcomes have no decisive result and no coverage.

The existing population runner now supports separate controller-owned family input/result schemas. It retains exact request/source/case inputs before computation, uses original source on interrupted retry, checks dependencies again before commitment and verifies case/draw bytes plus results in a fresh isolated process. The family schema participates in source-write protection, memory chronology and technical revocation ancestry. Per-operation time and 128 MiB stream bounds do not weaken resample requirements or supply a final-series budget.

Changed production files are `family_models.py`, `family_pipeline.py`, `population_runs.py`, `coordination.py`, `storage.py`, `memory.py` and `revocations.py`. The old population model, stationary-bootstrap and Monte Carlo implementations are preserved byte-for-byte. Focused tests and the independently authored `test_family_oracles.py` exercise this path.
