# Primary-source evidence register

Access date: October 24, 2026. Companion: [integration proposal](INTEGRATION_PROPOSAL.md).

This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive systematic review or meta-analysis. Search questions covered agent/tool orchestration, persistent memory and retrieval, agent evaluation, and adaptive scientific assessment. Searches used official OpenAI/Anthropic domains and paper titles with publisher/arXiv sources. Seven academic works and eight vendor documents were selected for direct relevance to the reconstructed framework. Older mechanism papers are not contemporary model rankings. Vendor guidance describes engineering practices and products; it does not independently establish research validity.

The register separates what a source supports from a project inference. Inspected sections are recorded rather than claiming every appendix was reviewed. Abstracts and search snippets alone were not used for substantive statistical recommendations. OpenReview's memory-paper download returned a browser challenge; its author-hosted arXiv manuscript was read instead. OpenAI Markdown pages were fetched directly after the browser parser rejected their content type. POPPER's publisher-linked PDF was read with a PDF parser after the browser tool rejected its binary content type. Extracted figure markup was not interpreted as a readable equation. No downloaded code was executed.

## Academic sources

| ID and primary source | Version and inspected material | Source-supported finding | Project inference and transfer limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 — Yao et al., [ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.03629) | ICLR 2023 paper; arXiv v3, March 10, 2023. Abstract, introduction and knowledge-intensive task setup. | The method interleaves model reasoning with task actions and observations; its demonstrations include external information retrieval and interactive tasks. | A researcher can choose useful queries dynamically. This does not authorize arbitrary tools, prove market discovery, or require recording private chain-of-thought. Evaluate observable requests, outputs and decisions. |
| A2 — Shinn et al., [Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/1b44b878bb782e6954cd888628510e90-Paper-Conference.pdf) | NeurIPS 2023. Abstract, section 3 opening, page 8 test-generation analysis and ablations. | Textual reflection on feedback is retained for subsequent attempts without weight updates. The paper also reports incorrect self-generated tests and a benchmark where the method underperforms its baseline. | Retain experience with its actual feedback and uncertainty. Reflection is a candidate heuristic, not an evidence upgrade; independent fixtures remain necessary. Coding/task results do not establish trading usefulness. |
| A3 — Wu et al., [LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813) | [ICLR 2025 publisher record](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d813d324dbf0598bbdc9c8e79740ed01-Abstract-Conference.html). Manuscript pages 3–7: task taxonomy, curation, evaluation, indexing/retrieval/reading and design controls. | Memory is evaluated across extraction, cross-session reasoning, updates, time and abstention. Retrieval and answer quality can be measured separately. Compression may discard needed details. | Test source recovery, contradictions, time scopes and justified unknown answers separately. Use raw records behind summaries. Conversational QA performance neither proves scientific memory correctness nor selects our database or ranking policy. |
| A4 — Liu et al., [Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9.pdf) | TACL 12, 2024, pp. 157–173; abstract and introduction. | The tested models' QA and key/value retrieval performance varies with relevant information's position in long contexts. | Do not assume that putting the complete archive in a large context solves retrieval. Test actual selected models and context layouts; this older study is not a universal performance claim about 2026 models. |
| A5 — Huang et al., [Automated Hypothesis Validation with Agentic Sequential Falsifications](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/huang25n.html) | ICML 2025, PMLR 267. Publisher abstract and [paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/huang25n/huang25n.pdf), sections 2.1–2.4, especially assumptions 1–3 and theorem 4, pp. 2–4. | POPPER combines designed falsification experiments using sequential evidence. Its guarantee requires null-implication validity, conditional validity of evidence and appropriate stopping; training independence is explicitly assumed. The paper states arbitrary LLMs do not automatically satisfy the conditions. | Useful methodological relative for explicit claims and checks. Its e-value procedure is not a replacement for our adopted fixed-batch contract. Unknown model training exposure and reused dependent market history require their own treatment. No guarantee transfers merely by running its agents. |
| A6 — Dwork et al., [Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/file/bad5f33780c42f2588878a9d07405083-Paper.pdf) | NeurIPS 2015; abstract, introduction and section 1.1, pp. 1–3. | Adaptively reusing evaluation information can overfit a holdout. The proposed reusable-holdout mechanisms have explicit information and sampling assumptions; their guarantees are not properties of ordinary repeated holdout access. | Treat feedback, retrieval and selection as part of the research process. Retaining lineage documents exposure; it does not itself remove adaptive bias. Do not transplant the paper's i.i.d. guarantees onto dependent market series. |
| A7 — Cawley and Talbot, [On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf) | JMLR 11, 2010, pp. 2079–2107. Abstract; sections 5.1–5.2, including internal versus median evaluation protocols. | Selection can overfit a noisy evaluation criterion. Evaluation that includes selection within the assessed procedure differs from evaluating a configuration selected with information from that assessment data. | Calibration and economic assessment must include the actual selection/feedback process when claiming process-level validity. The paper does not prescribe our temporal split, bootstrap, error budget or a universal sample size. |

## OpenAI primary documentation

These are living pages, accessed on the date above, rather than frozen API compatibility promises. Canonical HTML URLs are given; the corresponding `.md` representations were inspected directly. Exact SDK/model versions must be recorded at implementation and run time.

| ID and source | Inspected material | Source-supported finding | Project inference and transfer limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| V5 — [Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling) | Tool-calling steps; allowed tools; parallel calls and strict mode. | The application receives tool requests, executes functions and supplies results. Strict mode constrains arguments to a supported schema. | Implement a small host-owned loop and authorize every call independently. A model's valid tool name or JSON arguments are not access authorization or semantic correctness. Parallel execution is a policy choice, not a requirement. |
| V6 — [Structured model outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs) | Structured versus function outputs, refusals/incomplete responses, mistakes and schema consistency. | Structured output constrains form but can still contain mistakes. Refusal and incomplete responses require explicit handling. | Use it for draft records and AST proposals, followed by owned semantic checks and faithful-formalization review. It cannot turn a natural-language claim into a certified scientific experiment on its own. |
| V7 — [Sandboxes](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents/sandboxes) | Harness/compute separation, trusted services and distinct persistence surfaces. | Separates control-plane functions from model-directed execution, allowing narrow execution credentials while control remains outside it. | Keep the scientific controller and evaluator storage outside researcher tool access. The present trusted Python process is not an adversarial sandbox; initial tools should remain fixed broker functions. A sandbox is needed only for a separately scoped code-execution capability. |
| V8 — [Evaluate agent workflows](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agent-evals) | Main page: traces for diagnosis, datasets and repeated evaluations. | Traces expose tool/routing/instruction failures; repeatable datasets support workflow comparisons. | Add replayable agent and memory cases, record cost and failures, then compare actual model/prompt variants. These operational evals are separate from scientific calibration and economic evaluation. |

## Synthesis and readiness

The sources support a useful division: model-directed inquiry, persistent and selectively retrieved records, explicit execution control, and task-appropriate verification. The specific choice of the existing controller, SQLite projections, approved AST, scientific freeze and evaluation rules follows from this repository's accepted contracts and inspected code, not from a vendor endorsement.

No reviewed source establishes that a particular provider, multi-agent arrangement, reflection prompt or memory database will generate profitable hypotheses here. No benchmark percentages, universal thresholds, new stopping rule or extra agent count are imported. A production provider/model choice is not ready without the framework-specific evaluation and run inputs described in the proposal. The integration recommendation itself is ready as a reviewable design direction.
